# H3 Shot Planning

Write executable visual development, not a list of camera adjectives. Use the caller's existing content plan and H3 output format; do not add planning schemas, model calls or review gates.

## Preserve intent; develop the open space

User-specified actions, order, timing, framing, reference roles and prohibitions remain authoritative. A supplied opening is not necessarily a complete storyboard. For incomplete generation requests, use the minimum sufficient completion: develop actions, reactions and reveals needed to fulfill the intent without changing locked facts or outcomes. Freely choose execution details; add events only to bridge a causal gap, deliver a requested presentation, or establish the intended outcome. Grandeur or intensity alone does not justify a subplot, repeated climax or return to a reference composition. Explicit requests for broad invention expand this scope, but do not justify redundant coverage. A local edit, by contrast, preserves untouched source action and camera behavior; it is not permission to redesign the film.

Resolve contradictory requirements using the caller's conflict policy, not by silently dropping one. Do not promote a mood word, uncertain perception or optional cinematic idea into a mandatory effect.

Build a one-way content state chain before coverage. Each development changes a person, object, space, relationship, information, product state or requested visible result and continues from the state already reached. Do not invent cycles such as leave/return, complete/repeat, disappear/reappear, escalation/re-escalation or restoration of the opening reference composition unless the user requests a loop, repetition or return.

Aggregate by causality before listing developments. An anticipation, trigger, immediate reaction, intensity increase and direct result serving one goal are normally one continuous development. Brightening, louder sound, stronger shake, closer framing or increased tension does not split that chain. Create another development only for an independent result, a changed viewing purpose, or an important consequence the current space cannot show.

Separate semantic content from presentation. Actions, events, relationships, information, operations and visible outcomes are content. Framing, camera position, edit speed, lighting, color, grain, glitches, flashes, black frames, blur, shake and similar techniques are presentation. Presentation modifies content or a boundary; it does not independently justify a development, action, location, pose, ending or shot. A list of stylistic techniques does not require one occurrence of every item.

## Decide what changes before deciding where to cut

Distinguish an editorial cut from a new action phase, camera move or on-screen transformation:

- Fixed interfaces, continuous performances and local video edits can contain many events in one shot. Describe their ordered progression, not one shot per event.
- In a montage or narrative, cut when the next view changes what the viewer can understand or feel: a reveal, reaction, spatial relationship, scale, time/place, product function, or a rhythmic accent grounded in a concrete action or requested pattern. Generic intensity is not a sufficient reason for another view. Honor explicitly requested cuts even if another treatment is possible.
- Returning to the same person or subject can advance an exchange or musical rhythm. Remove redundant coverage, not purposeful repetition. A different angle alone does not require another shot.

Choose the structure from the task, not a default shot count. Do not stretch a brief opening pose across an otherwise open film; do not fragment a continuous dance into decorative coverage.

Choose one primary editorial grammar for the film from the user's intent: continuous narrative, fashion montage, impact trailer, sequential keyframe transfer, or an explicitly requested alternative. Let shot density, boundary treatment and rhythm follow that grammar instead of inventing a different transition at every cut. Smoothness is not universal: preserve requested hard cuts, black frames, flashes and deliberate discontinuity.

Then choose task-adaptive coverage rather than a fixed shot template. Narrative and action cut when the viewing purpose changes. Product and visual showcase work may move among non-overlapping presentation dimensions such as the whole object, structure or operation, material detail, silhouette and a final hero view. Dance, performance, complex continuous operation and choreography that depends on positional relationships should remain continuous when one composition can show them clearly. Explicit storyboards, reference edit structures and keyframe orders control their locked scope. A mixed task may combine these logics, but each cut serves the current dominant purpose.

Words such as fast, dynamic, cinematic, commercial, montage or impactful do not authorize more events or shots by themselves. Keep the content state chain economical, then express pace through duration, framing contrast, coherent camera movement and a small consistent set of boundary treatments. Every short shot still needs content or presentation value that adjacent coverage cannot deliver clearly.

Before adding a shot, compare its viewing benefit with the continuity cost of re-establishing subject, space and action. Cut only when the new view supplies content or presentation value the current shot cannot present clearly: an important action, relationship, scale, time/place, product function, distinct structure/material/silhouette feature, result, emotional distance, or requested rhythm. A product detail may earn a shot when it reveals a distinct feature that the main view cannot show; it must not replay an operation or repeat a feature already established. A reaction or distant resolving view is not automatically a separate shot. A new angle alone is not a benefit.

When the user permits invention, create restrained viewpoint variety across the whole film by assigning different viewing purposes where the content needs them: spatial establishment, participation in an action, relationship, essential detail, result reveal or emotional change. Do not try to include every purpose or mechanically cycle through wide, medium, close, high and low angles. Change angle only when observation target, spatial relation, scale, action information or emotional distance changes meaningfully. If another angle merely repeats a readable action or state, keep the shot and use subject movement, reframing, pan, track or a gradual scale change. Across a continuous action phrase, preserve screen direction, camera side and spatial relations; an earned new view resumes the action in progress instead of replaying it.

## Use each reference in its actual role

Identity sheets, scene boards, style references, action phases and chronological storyboards are different inputs. Multiple panels may supply one character, one world or several connected views. Multiple pictures may belong to one continuous shot. When the user requests the supplied storyboard's shot sequence and rhythm, preserve its separate views and order rather than merging them for economy. Otherwise picture count alone does not determine shot count.

Place a keyframe where its action state belongs: first frame, intermediate beat, last frame, or composition reference. Do not start from the completed result when the user asks to show its arrival. Preserve the selected scene's recognizable anchors without carrying unrequested panel borders or unrelated identities into the frame.

For video reference, inherit only requested dimensions: choreography, performance rhythm, camera path, edit structure or scene. Describe the important observed action sequence rather than relying solely on “exactly follow the video.” Do not fabricate cadence from missing evidence or prolong the source simply because the output allows more time; honor the requested duration and continuation policy.

## Build a continuous, visible action phrase

Plan a complete action phrase before deciding cuts: preparation → action → visible consequence. In the same space, prefer to keep a continuous operation, brief interpersonal exchange or continuous departure in one shot, using performance, reframing or coherent camera movement to reveal its stages. The phrase may span shots when a new view earns the cut; each shot need not complete the action or end in a hold. Do not automatically split an operation into preparation, hand detail and resulting pose, or a departure into tracking coverage and a second wide proof of departure. Prefer an uninterrupted view when a cut adds no information, presentation value or requested rhythm, without forcing a single-take style. The camera should make that development readable. A prop handoff needs an owner after the transfer; a reaction follows its trigger; an intermediate pose need not become a static hold.

Choose framing that shows the relevant action: bodies and floor contact for choreography, both sides of a relationship when needed, or the product surface during its use. Integrate commercial detail into handling, movement and material response when the request calls for a campaign rather than a catalogue.

Use a static camera when it serves observation, tension or a locked layout. For movement, describe a coherent path and the reveal it enables; sequential moves are valid when their cues and direction are clear. A focus change is not a camera translation. Avoid simultaneously calling the camera fixed and moving. Do not add an orbit, shake or push to every shot.

## Make boundaries specific, not louder

For continuous time and space, treat the outgoing end state as the incoming starting condition. When flow is wanted, cut during an action and resume its unfinished phase, rather than stop and restart. Record the cut instant in end_state; it may be mid-action. Preserve intentional pauses, static displays, ellipses and discontinuities. At a cut, retain the relevant screen direction, eyeline, object ownership, wardrobe and action state. Keep relative positions and the camera side understandable; a change of side, place or time needs a readable visual cue. Do not restart a completed action or silently skip a user-required transformation. Ellipsis is valid when the omitted action is not required and the resulting state is understandable.

Give each boundary one primary concrete connection in existing action or sound fields: action match, eyeline, cause/reaction, composition, sound bridge, or an intentional time/place jump. Do not stack techniques or substitute “smooth transition” for a concrete connection. Keep only executable transition details in H3, without adding fields or explanatory essays. Short shots should form a readable action phrase rather than repeatedly restart it. Black frames and flashes are purposeful punctuation, not default connectors or separate filler shots; preserve explicitly requested independent black shots. An ordinary editorial cut needs no invented optical effect. When a stylized transition is wanted, name the actual bridge: outgoing motion or shape, brief occlusion/blur or transformation, and the incoming view that resolves it. Describe it once at the boundary rather than applying flash, refocus or shake throughout every content shot. For blur, flash, smoke, darkness, occlusion or a foreground wipe that conceals a source/scene replacement, make the boundary atomic and irreversible: fully conceal the outgoing content, perform the source swap at peak concealment, then reveal only the incoming content. The outgoing scene must not regain clarity after the swap. Persistent stylistic effects are retained when requested. A UI selection, curtain opening or local material change is not automatically a full-frame transition.

Allocate effects by function: persistent global texture, change within a shot, or treatment at a boundary. Select the combination needed for the intent; do not add coverage merely to accommodate an effect list. As a final counterfactual check, remove camera and effect words from each proposed shot. If no necessary new content or presentation value remains, merge its technique into adjacent coverage. If two adjacent actions can trade places without changing the state chain, they are likely redundant coverage. Also compare adjacent viewing purposes. Merge consecutive shots that differ only by framing or camera position while repeating the same action, reaction, result or presentation feature. For product and visual showcase work, identify a distinct presentation dimension for each view; whole form, structure or operation, material detail, silhouette and hero composition may differ, but multiple views must not prove the same feature again. If an open design uses one observation distance throughout and an important space, relation, operation or result remains unreadable from it, add one view that supplies that specific information. Multiple shots may share a development only when each contributes necessary non-overlapping information and resumes the reached action state.

## Allocate time to content

Honor explicit timing and evidenced reference cadence first. Within open time, allow the central action and its readable outcome to happen before budgeting secondary poses, reactions or an ending. Choose shot count from that allocation. Equal durations can serve a beat; unequal durations are not inherently better. Do not use a fixed main-action percentage or mandatory walking/branding template.

Preserve a requested ending or keyframe. Otherwise resolve the current development or leave an appropriate hook; do not invent a logo, title, factual claim or new plot just to end the clip. Check whether each pause serves the content or merely closes a shot. Remove only the latter and carry the action through the boundary. Avoid long decorative holds that displace the requested performance.

Once the principal action has produced a clear result, hold it within the current shot or move directly to the requested ending. Do not automatically add a distant confirmation shot, a final pose, a return to the reference composition, or another title reveal merely to prove the result again.

## Keep the final prompt economical

State stable source bindings and global style once in their existing sections. In each shot, write the visible action phrase, necessary framing/camera behavior and changed or continuity-critical details. Repeat a product or appearance anchor only where it materially prevents loss, not every attribute in every paragraph. Keep timing consistent with the caller's target. Editorial notes belong in existing planning fields where supported, not in the executable H3 prose.
